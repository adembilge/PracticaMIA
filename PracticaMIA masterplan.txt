Stage 1 — Product Discovery and Definition
Tasks 1–6 · Establish the product's purpose, users, scope, and success criteria.
Task 1 — Define the product vision
Implementation: Codex examines the PaperPilot concept and develops a concise product strategy. It identifies the central problem, the intended audience, the benefits to users, and why an autonomous AI agent is necessary rather than a conventional chatbot or static checklist. It should also identify what PaperPilot must not attempt to do.

Deliverable: A product vision document containing the mission, value proposition, target outcomes, guiding principles, and long-term direction.

Completion: The document clearly explains who PaperPilot serves, which problem it solves, and what successful use looks like. You approve the vision.
Task 2 — Research user problems
Implementation: Codex investigates common challenges faced by international residents, students, and professionals navigating Italian bureaucracy. It reviews available public evidence, identifies frequent frustrations, and prepares interview questions for potential users. It must distinguish documented problems from assumptions requiring user validation.

Deliverable: A problem-research report containing user pain points, supporting evidence, initial hypotheses, and a research interview guide.

Completion: A prioritized list of problems exists, with supporting sources or explicit validation needs. Codex must not invent user interviews or claim uncollected feedback.
Task 3 — Analyze existing solutions
Implementation: Codex researches existing government websites, digital administrative platforms, document-preparation services, and AI-based assistance products. It compares their capabilities, limitations, intended audiences, languages, and user workflows. It identifies where PaperPilot could provide additional value through personalized case management and document preparation.

Deliverable: A competitor matrix and a differentiation analysis.

Completion: The report identifies concrete opportunities and explains why PaperPilot would be meaningfully different from existing services.
Task 4 — Define MVP boundaries
Implementation: Codex translates the product vision into a tightly scoped first release. It specifies supported users, locations, residence-change scenarios, languages, document types, and agent actions. It explicitly excludes unsupported immigration, legal, family, and government-submission workflows.

Deliverable: An MVP specification with included features, excluded features, assumptions, and a definition of a complete user journey.

Completion: Every proposed feature can be classified as included, excluded, or deferred. You approve the MVP scope.
Task 5 — Define user scenarios
Implementation: Codex prepares realistic administrative scenarios representing different users and situations. These should include successful cases, missing documents, contradictory information, unsupported requests, and uncertain eligibility. Each scenario defines the user's initial request, available information, expected questions, and acceptable outcome.

Deliverable: A structured scenario dataset that can later become the foundation for agent evaluations.

Completion: Scenarios cover major supported decision branches and important failure conditions. Expected outcomes are marked as provisional until the governing requirements are verified.
Task 6 — Write the product specification
Implementation: Codex consolidates the approved research, product vision, target audience, MVP scope, user scenarios, functional requirements, and nonfunctional requirements. It assigns stable identifiers to requirements so future implementation tasks can reference them.

Deliverable: docs/PRODUCT.md, containing the complete product requirements specification.

Completion: The specification is internally consistent, traceable to previous decisions, and approved by you. It becomes the authoritative product reference.

Stage 1 completion: PaperPilot has a clear purpose, validated assumptions are distinguished from unvalidated ones, and its first release is explicitly defined.

Stage 2 — Government Procedure Research
Tasks 7–14 · Establish reliable administrative knowledge before implementing the AI agent.

It identifies required starting conditions, actions, decision points, submission channels, follow-up steps, and expected completion outcomes. Different official routes must remain separate where their requirements differ.

Deliverable: A procedural workflow diagram and structured sequence of administrative actions.

Completion: Every supported workflow path has a documented beginning, required steps, decision branches, and submission endpoint.
Task 9 — Identify eligibility scenarios
Implementation: Codex identifies which user characteristics can change the applicable administrative procedure. Examples may include current registration, relocation origin, citizenship, household composition, residence documentation, and housing arrangement. It builds an explicit decision table rather than leaving eligibility decisions to unrestricted LLM reasoning.

Deliverable: An eligibility matrix containing conditions, branches, exceptions, and source references.

Completion: Each supported scenario maps to a defined procedural route, while unsupported or ambiguous scenarios lead to an explicit unresolved state.
Task 10 — Extract document requirements
Implementation: Codex examines official instructions and records every document or information field required for supported scenarios. It differentiates mandatory, conditional, alternative, and optional documents. It also records whether an original, copy, specific form, or additional declaration is required.

Deliverable: A structured document-requirements catalog linked to official sources.

Completion: Each requirement includes its applicability conditions, evidence, and review status. Unsupported assumptions are not accepted as requirements.
Task 11 — Identify deadlines and obligations
Implementation: Codex researches applicable submission deadlines, processing stages, fees where relevant, and possible related obligations. It records the triggering event, deadline calculation, authority, and exceptions. It distinguishes fixed rules from guidance or service estimates that may change.

Deliverable: A deadline and obligations registry.

Completion: Every deadline used by the MVP is traceable to an approved source, with ambiguous or conflicting rules flagged for review.
Task 12 — Research submission channels
Implementation: Codex determines how each supported application is officially submitted. It records applicable portals, identity verification methods, required attachments, confirmation mechanisms, and whether an authorized machine integration exists. It explicitly defines where PaperPilot's responsibilities end and the user's official submission begins.

Deliverable: A submission-channel specification and handoff workflow.

Completion: Each supported route has a verified destination and documented user actions. No unsupported automatic submission capability is assumed.
Task 13 — Identify related procedures
Implementation: Codex investigates administrative obligations potentially connected to a residence change, such as local taxation or updates to other records. It determines which are directly dependent, which merely warrant user awareness, and which should remain outside the MVP.

Deliverable: A dependency map and related-procedures catalog.

Completion: Related obligations are classified without accidentally expanding the MVP into additional unsupported procedures.
Task 14 — Validate administrative research
Implementation: Codex audits the complete research package for conflicting information, outdated pages, missing source references, and inconsistent decision rules. It prepares a reviewable dossier containing all conclusions and unresolved questions. You, or an appropriately qualified reviewer where necessary, validate consequential administrative requirements.

Deliverable: A versioned, reviewed administrative knowledge dossier.

Completion: All requirements intended for production are approved or marked unresolved. Unresolved critical rules block dependent development or release.

Stage 2 completion: The project has a reviewed, source-grounded description of the supported administrative procedure.

Task 20 — Prepare the UI design system

Deliverable: Design tokens, component specifications, UI prototypes, and a design-system reference.

Completion: The design system supports all approved screens and major interaction states. You approve the design direction.

Stage 3 completion: PaperPilot has a coherent, reviewed user experience that can be implemented without inventing interface requirements during development.
