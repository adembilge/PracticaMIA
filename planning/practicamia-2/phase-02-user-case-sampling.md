## Sampling targets

Recruit people who recently attempted a specific administrative task in Italy. Use one primary recruitment group per case for counting and add secondary group labels where useful. Start with one case per participant. If a participant recounts two distinct tasks, give each a case ID and link both to the same participant code so the participant count remains accurate.

| **Total** | **20** | |

Check coverage across at least three regions, with cases from northern, central and southern Italy or the islands. Include large cities and smaller municipalities. Capture self-reported confidence with administrative Italian, ranging from needing help to handling it independently. Include people who complete digital procedures independently, use them with assistance, or rely on phone or in-person channels. These are starting targets. Review gaps after the first five cases and direct the remaining outreach towards missing profiles. Record any gap that remains when collection ends.

## Screening and intake

1. Ask the person to name a specific administrative task they personally attempted in Italy, where it took place, and roughly when they last acted or heard back. Use the [case outcome instructions](#record-the-case-outcome) to establish its stage. Keep the location at the level needed to understand the procedure.
2. Ask which recruitment group applies, how comfortable they were with the Italian used in the process, and whether they could use the digital service independently. Offer a conversation in Italian or English and a phone or in-person option where available.
3. Explain the purpose of the study and ask permission to record de-identified notes. Keep contact details and permission records in restricted storage separate from the case cards. Use the approved research process for any optional follow-up or redacted evidence. Keep completed case cards, raw notes, documents and screenshots out of GitHub.
4. Count a case once the participant has described a distinct task they attempted, agreed to the notes, and provided enough detail to identify the stage and obstacle. Record an unknown authority or unavailable evidence explicitly. Keep the number of participants and the number of eligible cases visible as separate totals.

## Record the case outcome

Record the stage at the time of the conversation for the participant's stated goal. Capture the route in the order it happened, including any switch between authorities or channels that changed what the participant could do next.

| Stage | When to use it | What to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Completed | The participant reached the outcome they set out to achieve. | The route from the first action to the result, including any detour and how they knew the goal was reached. |
| Ongoing | The participant is still working towards the goal, is waiting for a response, or plans to resume after a pause. | The last action they took, what remains uncertain, and what they expect to do next or are waiting for. Mark an unknown next step explicitly. |
| Abandoned | The participant stopped the attempt and reports no current plan to resume it. | The last action they took, the point and reason they stopped if known, and any workaround they used. |

Ask what the participant set out to achieve when they say they are done. If the goal was a decision and the participant is waiting for it after submitting, record the case as ongoing and the submission as the last action. Identify participant-reported milestones as such and note corroborating evidence when available. When the stage stays unclear, keep the intake pending and report it separately from eligible cases. If a participant later reports a change, update the same case ID and retain the earlier stage and observation period.

## Case card template

```markdown
### Case ID [C-___]
- Participant code [P-___]
- Primary recruitment group / secondary groups
- Location and jurisdiction (only the detail needed)
- Confidence with administrative Italian / digital service use
- Opening sentence (participant's words, with identifiers removed)
- Authority (as reported or unknown)
- Stage (completed / ongoing / abandoned)
- Observation period (approximate) and actions or channels in order
- Stage detail (route and outcome signal / last action and next uncertainty / stopping point and reason)
- Obstacle
- Evidence (participant account or permitted redacted material; restricted reference if available)
- Current workaround
- Expected outcome (what the participant was trying to achieve)
```
