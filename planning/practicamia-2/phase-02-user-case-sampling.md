## Screening and intake

1. Ask the person to name a specific administrative task they personally attempted in Italy, where it took place, and roughly when they last acted or heard back. Use the [case outcome instructions](#record-the-case-outcome) to establish its stage. Keep the location at the level needed to understand the procedure.
2. Ask which recruitment group applies, how comfortable they were with the Italian used in the process, and whether they could use the digital service independently. Offer a conversation in Italian or English and a phone or in-person option where available.
3. Explain the purpose of the study and ask permission to record de-identified notes. Keep contact details and permission records in restricted storage separate from the case cards. Use the approved research process for any optional follow-up or redacted evidence. Keep completed case cards, raw notes, documents and screenshots out of GitHub.
4. Count a case once the participant has described a distinct task they attempted, agreed to the notes, and provided enough detail to identify the stage and obstacle. Record an unknown authority or unavailable evidence explicitly. Keep the number of participants and the number of eligible cases visible as separate totals.

## Core interview questions

Use the same questions in this order for each case. After the participant agrees to de-identified notes, ask the opening question before suggesting a procedure, authority or possible obstacle. If screening has already covered the goal, invite the participant to describe it again in their own words.

| English | Italiano | Case card |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Think of a specific administrative task you tried to do in Italy. What were you trying to do? | Ripensi a una pratica amministrativa che ha cercato di svolgere in Italia. Che cosa stava cercando di fare? | Opening sentence, expected outcome |
| What did you do first? | Qual è stata la prima cosa che ha fatto? | First action |
| Which office or service did you turn to, if any? How did you choose it? | A quale ufficio o servizio si è rivolto, se lo ha fatto? Come ha fatto a sceglierlo? | Authority as reported and how it was chosen |
| Where, if anywhere, did it become hard to continue? What happened at that point? | C’è stato un momento in cui è stato difficile andare avanti? Che cosa è successo in quel momento? | Obstacle and point in the route |
| Who did you ask for help, if anyone? What happened after you asked? | A chi ha chiesto aiuto, se lo ha fatto? Che cosa è successo dopo aver chiesto aiuto? | Help sought and workaround |
| Where does the task stand now? Is anything left to do? | A che punto è la pratica adesso? C’è ancora qualcosa da fare? | Stage, result and what remains |

Write down the opening sentence in the participant’s original language before asking follow-ups. Preserve their wording and replace identifying details with `[redacted]`. Add any translation on a separate line. If you could only capture a summary, label it as a paraphrase. Use neutral follow-ups such as “What happened next?” or “Che cosa è successo dopo?” to clarify the order of events. Use the [outcome instructions](#record-the-case-outcome) to record stage-specific details. If an answer is missing, distinguish between a participant reporting none and an answer that was unclear or unavailable.

## Record the case outcome

Record the stage at the time of the conversation for the participant's stated goal. Capture the route in the order it happened, including any switch between authorities or channels that changed what the participant could do next.

| Stage | When to use it | What to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Completed | The participant reached the outcome they set out to achieve. | The route from the first action to the result, including any detour and how they knew the goal was reached. |
| Ongoing | The participant is still working towards the goal, is waiting for a response, or plans to resume after a pause. | The last action they took, what remains uncertain, and what they expect to do next or are waiting for. Mark an unknown next step explicitly. |
| Abandoned | The participant stopped the attempt and reports no current plan to resume it. | The last action they took, the point and reason they stopped if known, and any workaround they used. |

Ask what the participant set out to achieve when they say they are done. If the goal was a decision and the participant is waiting for it after submitting, record the case as ongoing and the submission as the last action. Identify participant-reported milestones as such and note corroborating evidence when available. When the stage stays unclear, keep the intake pending and report it separately from eligible cases. If a participant later reports a change, update the same case ID and retain the earlier stage and observation period.

## Reconstruct steps with redacted evidence

1. Start with the participant’s account. Ask permission to view each relevant redacted official notice, portal screenshot, form or receipt and record de-identified observations. Ask separately before retaining a copy and follow the approved research handling process. Viewing and taking notes are sufficient for this step. If permission is declined or the artifact is unavailable, continue with the participant account and record the reason the evidence is unavailable if known.
2. Use only the pages or screen area needed for the step. Before viewing, ask the participant to remove names, contact details, addresses, identity and case numbers, barcodes or QR codes, and session links or tokens. If identifying information is still visible or recoverable in the file’s text, layers or metadata, set the copy aside. Keep any permitted copy and related notes in restricted research storage. Use an internal evidence ID in the case card when a copy is retained.
3. Match the displayed office, service and document context to the case with the participant. For each relevant artifact record its type, the page or screen section, its place in the timeline, and the minimum visible words or controls that explain the step. Mark an unreadable passage, unclear date, or uncertain match as unresolved. Leave an unmatched item out of the corroborated timeline until the match is clear.
4. Ask what the participant understood at the time and what they did afterwards. Give the visible content, the participant’s interpretation, and their reported action each its own field. Keep any disagreement or missing context visible in the record. A receipt supports only the event it explicitly acknowledges. Record any authority decision from evidence that actually shows the decision.

## Case card template

```markdown
### Case ID [C-___]
- Participant code [P-___]
- Primary recruitment group / secondary groups
- Location and jurisdiction (only the detail needed)
- Confidence with administrative Italian / digital service use
- Opening sentence (original language, with identifiers removed) and optional labelled translation
- Authority (as reported or unknown) and how the participant chose it
- Stage (completed / ongoing / abandoned)
- Observation period (approximate), first action and subsequent actions or channels in order
- Stage detail (route and outcome signal / last action and next uncertainty / stopping point and reason)
- Obstacle
- Evidence status (participant account only or redacted material viewed, with a restricted evidence ID if retained)
- Evidence by step (type, page or screen section, what was visible, participant interpretation, reported action and unresolved gaps)
- Help sought (role or service) and current workaround
- Expected outcome (what the participant was trying to achieve)
```
